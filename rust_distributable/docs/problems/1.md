# Problem 1: three refused attacks, a 24 h account lock, and 13 pointless re-logins

Run of 7 Oct 21:24 to 8 Oct 05:21 (service API v41). Written 8 Oct from the live
database and the service's HTTP status. Nothing here was changed in code yet.

## Verdict

It was **not** map scanning (`gaa`), a stale map, or a request flood. In order:

1. **The bot sent three attacks the castle could not supply.** The attack asks for
   50 crossbows (unit 607). The castle had **6, then 12** at home. The server
   refused each `cra` with `status 101`.
2. **The bot treated 101 as a normal "this tower said no" and carried on.** It took
   another commander and another tower 15 s later, twice more.
3. **On the third 101 (04:51:24) the server dropped the connection and locked the
   account for 24 hours** (login now answers `status 27` until 04:51:24 on 9 Oct).
4. **My reconnect supervisor then logged in 13 more times in 30 minutes** against
   that lock, every attempt answered `27`, and gave up with a message that hides
   why. That regular back and forth is the "suspicious" pattern you saw, and it is
   a real bug, not noise.

Steps 1 and 2 caused the lock. Step 4 made the aftermath look worse and is the
reason the log is full of connection attempts.

## What the evidence is, and what it is not

* The daemon writes no log file (its `tracing` output goes to stdout, which an
  installed app discards). The only record is SQLite.
* `network_message` keeps the **latest 200 rows**. Those rows start at 04:50:34, so
  the minute before the first refusal is gone. Everything below that cites a row
  quotes `sequence` numbers from that window.
* Outbound `lli` (the login packet, which carries credentials) is deliberately not
  recorded. The logins are visible only through the server's `lli` replies and
  the frames around them.
* The ledger (`attack_ledger`) and `commander_state` are complete and were used for
  rates and the last successful attack.

## 1. Why the attacks were refused

`gui.I` in the ADI reply is the attacking castle's home inventory, and `gui.TU` is
the number of troops currently out. These are **troop counts, not marches**: 373
crossbows out is about seven or eight sand armies of 50, not 373 marches. At the
moment of the lock only 12 commanders were out (9 outbound, 3 returning), and the
highest commander LID ever used is 42 (35 distinct), so nothing ran away.

| Unit 607 (crossbow) | Home (`gui.I`) | Out (`gui.TU`), troops | Needed by one sand attack |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 04:50:43 | 6 | 373 | 50 |
| 04:51:02 | 12 | 359 | 50 |
| 04:51:20 | 12 | 359 | 50 |

Earlier snapshots of the same figure, from the `adi` replies that survive in older
copies of the database:

| Time | Home 607 | Out 607 | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 7 Oct 10:51 | 22,842 | 85 | 22,927 |
| 7 Oct 12:41 | 21,459 | 853 | 22,312 |
| 8 Oct 04:50 | 6 | 373 | 379 |

So the castle held about **22,000 crossbows sixteen hours earlier and 379 at the end**.
Between 12:41 and 04:48 the sand task sent **1,177 attacks** (about 73 an hour for
16 hours). 21,933 crossbows gone over 1,177 attacks is **about 18.6 lost per
attack**, which matches a loss of roughly 20 per won attack. Winning an RBC still
costs troops (the seven `cat` replies on record returned 31 to 38 of 50). Nothing
mysterious or runaway happened: 1,177 attacks x about 19 is 22,000. The stock was
simply never replaced at the sending castle (recruiting was running, but its queue
at Castle Ventrilo is 845 per 12,738 s, about 240 an hour, against a drain of about
1,400 an hour).

Two limits on that figure: the early stretch is much gentler (87 attacks between 10:51
and 12:41 cost a net 615, about 7 each, with recruiting and returning troops mixed
in), and I have no stock readings between 12:41 and 04:50, so the exact rate over the
night is an average. The 8 Oct total of 379 is also why 17 commanders were free and
the castle still could not field one army. Commanders were free; troops were not.

The Python bot never sent such an attack. After every ADI it stored `gui.I` and
before the CRA ran `army_shortfall(attack_payload, inventory)` (`bot/packets.py`,
used at `bot/rbc_proxy_listener.py` around line 3100). A shortfall meant
`proxy_cra_skip reason=army_short unit=607 need=50 have=12`: the attack was skipped
and nothing was sent. **The Rust port had no equivalent** (`gui` was not read
anywhere in `crates/`); v42 adds one, see "Fixed" below.

What `101` means: it is the same "the army is not there" class that Python files
under `class=army` (Berimond: "CRA 101/313 -> army"). 313 is the documented
"not enough troops"; 101 shows up with `reason=unknown` and, in 109 Python log
lines: 107 on Berimond and 2 on `storm`, none on Sands. This is its first appearance
on Sands. That the
home count is below the request on all three refusals fits, but the server does
not say so itself, so treat "insufficient troops" as the best-supported reading
rather than a certainty.

## 2. Why it kept going

`Automation::observe` (`direct/automation.rs`) handles any non-zero status on a
CRA the same way:

* tower: parked for one hour (status other than 95);
* commander: released after `COMMANDER_REJECT_HOLD_MS` = 15 s;
* **no operational error is recorded** (the code comment says game errors such as
  95 or 93 are expected);
* phase returns to `Idle` and the scheduler picks the next tower.

`errors.md` does not list 101, so it falls into that default. Result: after each
101 the pipeline was healthy again 15 s later and sent the next one. The existing
safety pause (two operational errors in five minutes) never fired because refusals
are deliberately excluded from it.

Compare Python's policy, which would have stopped on the second: for CRA errors it
tolerated at most 2 per 10 minutes (`max_errors=2 window=600s`), then stayed
stopped, and its Berimond controller backed off 180 s on an "army" class refusal.

## 3. The lock

* `lli` status `27` with payload `{"GDPR":0,"RS":<seconds>}`; `RS` decreases in step
  with wall time and every reply resolves to the same instant, 9 Oct 04:51:24.
  That is a fixed expiry, not a per-attempt penalty.
* The expiry coincides with the third `101` (reply at 04:51:24.682). The most
  likely trigger is the server's own protection against repeated invalid
  requests, i.e. the third bad `cra` in 40 s. I can show the coincidence and the
  exact 24 h; I cannot see the server's rule, and a different cause that landed on
  the same second is very unlikely but not excluded.
* The earlier history is consistent with that: the Python bot tolerated two 101s per
  ten minutes for weeks (109 of them) and was never locked.

Each attempt is a full fresh handshake: open the socket, `rlu`, `vck`, `lli` with
credentials, answer `27`. The 160 s spacing is not a design choice; it falls out
of two things interacting:

1. `session.rs` treats a non-zero `lli` as `phase = Failed` but returns `Ok`, not an
   error. The socket stays open and nothing ends the session.
2. The server then says nothing more, so my 90 s silence watchdog (added for the
   Wi-Fi drop) fires, raises `ConnectionLost`, and the supervisor backs off
   (capped at 60 s, plus about 3 s jitter) and logs in again: 90 + 60 + about 10
   is about 160 s.

I wrote "a rejected login is final, never retried", but the rejection never became an
error, so the rule could not apply. The intended behaviour was right and the
detection was wrong.

Three further defects stack on that:

* **The reason is hidden.** On giving up, the supervisor wraps the error with
  `.context("gave up reconnecting")`, and the status shows only that text. Nothing
  in the UI says "locked until 04:51 tomorrow".
* **It looks like probing.** Thirteen credential logins at regular intervals
  against an account the server has just locked is the kind of pattern a server
  could read as password guessing or a reconnect storm. I cannot tell whether it
  extended the lock (the expiry never moved, so it did not lengthen it), but it
  added risk for no benefit.
* **The first reconnect came 7 s after a server-side drop that followed three
  protocol-level refusals.** Reconnecting immediately after being kicked is the
  worst moment to do it.

## Checked: was it the wrong commanders again?

No. The three refused CRAs all carried **LID 8**, which is human 6, inside the
crossbow block (human 1 to 17, LIDs 0 to 24). Evidence:

* LID 8 made two ordinary sand attacks earlier that night with the identical
  payload (04:15 and 04:34, both acknowledged and returned), and was home and
  available when it was used (`commander_state`: `available` from 04:51:39; its
  04:34 march returned at 04:43:53).
* The refusals did not vary with the commander: same LID, same payload, three
  different towers, same `101`. The only thing that changed between the
  successful 04:48:27 attack and the first refusal at 04:50:46 is the crossbow stock
  at home (at least 50, then 6).
* Since the v41 build started (21:24) the ledger shows **0** crossbow attacks with a
  second-section commander and 0 the other way, over 619 attacks. Strict allocation
  held.

The earlier bad-commander problem was real but is a different, older fault:
between 12:39 and 20:34 on 7 Oct, while the lending build (v40) was installed,
**225 crossbow attacks went out under LIDs 25 to 42** (18 different commanders)
and 6 second-section attacks under crossbow LIDs. That stopped when v41 was
installed. It did not cause the 101s, but it may have fed the troop shortage
indirectly: lending let the crossbow task run 35 commanders instead of 17 for
about eight hours, so it spent crossbows much faster. I cannot prove that link;
the stock figures from that period are not retained.

## What it was not

* **Not `gaa`/map traffic.** The only client commands in the last 40 s before the
  drop are `adi` and `cra`. The `gaa` rows in the window are server pushes after a
  landing (`[2,597,574,-1,112,10800,1]`); the cooldown-refresh phase I added sent
  none. The ADI reply itself contains the target's map row, so no extra tile read
  was needed.
* **Not request rate.** Three ADI+CRA pairs in 40 s is the normal handshake cadence
  (the 4 s CRA floor plus the post-ack pause); the hourly rate (58 in the 04:00 hour) was
  below the night's average.
* **Not the network.** Reconnect and server replies were immediate.
* **Not commander allocation.** See the section above.

## Fixed (API v42 and v43)

Fixes 1 to 5 below are implemented; 6 and 7 are not. All have tests.

* **Army pre-flight** (`hunt::home_inventory`, `army_shortfall`): the ADI reply's
  `gui.I` is checked against what the attack payload asks for. A shortfall sends
  nothing, releases the tower, and waits 30 to 60 s with
  `Waiting for troops to return: unit 607 need 50 have 12`.
* **Unknown CRA refusal stops the mode.** Only 95, 93 and 256 are routine; any
  other status (101, 313, 5, ...) turns attacks off and keeps `STOPPED: the server
  refused an attack with status N...` on the status line until the mode is
  started again.
* **A refused login is final.** Any non-zero `lli` ends the session with
  `LoginRejected`. For `27` the server's expiry (`RS`) is stored under
  `login.locked_until_ms.<account>` and every connect, including after an app
  restart, refuses locally until it passes. The lock record is cleared on the next
  successful login.
* **The real reason is shown.** The final status error keeps the whole chain.
* **Two retries, then stop.** About 1 minute after a loss and about 5 minutes after
  that (each +/-20 %), then give up. Down from retrying for 30 minutes.
* **History is kept (v43).** Troop stock per castle, troops sent / returned / lost
  per attack, and connection and refusal events, all per account and bounded; see
  `docs/architecture/database.md`. The status line shows stock and burn rate.

## What to do now

* **Do not log this account in before 9 Oct 04:51:24 local.** Every attempt before
  then is answered `27` and adds nothing. The service has stopped retrying; if the
  app is restarted and re-initialised it will attempt a login again, so avoid
  reconnecting until then.
* Check the castle's crossbow count and recruitment before the next run. The sand
  task cannot sustain 17 commanders at 50 troops each without recruiting.

## Recommended fixes (not implemented)

1. **Pre-flight army check, ported from Python.** *(done)* Parse `gui.I` from every ADI and
   compare it with the payload's required units before building the CRA; on a
   shortfall skip with `army_short unit=607 need=50 have=12` and release the target
   and commander. This alone would have prevented all three refusals.
2. **Treat an unknown CRA status as a hard stop.** *(done)* Known benign refusals (95, 256,
   93) keep today's handling; anything else (101, 313, 5, 90, unknown) stops
   attacks after one occurrence, or at most two per ten minutes as Python did, and
   says why in the status line. Add 101 to `errors.md`.
3. **A rejected login is terminal.** *(done)* On any non-zero `lli`, end the session with a
   typed error. For `27`, read `RS`, store `locked_until`, show it, and refuse to
   connect (including after an app restart) until it passes.
4. **Keep the root cause visible.** *(done)* Stop wrapping the final error in a way that
   replaces its text; keep the last server status in `DirectStatus.error`.
5. **Slow the first reconnect after a server-initiated close** *(done: 1 min then 5 min, two retries only)* that follows
   refusals (for example 60 s minimum) and cap logins per hour.
6. **Make connection events durable.** *(done, v43)* Record each connect, login result, close and
   reason in a small table (or a rolling log file), so this analysis does not
   depend on a 200-row window.
7. **Make the scheduler troop-aware.** Cap concurrent sand marches by
   `gui.I[607] / 50` plus returns expected, not only by free commanders.

## Open questions

* Exactly what the server rule behind the lock is (third invalid `cra`, or a
  threshold of invalid requests per window). Only the server can answer; the safe
  assumption is that one invalid army is already too many.
* Whether `101` always means "army unavailable" or has other causes. Python filed
  all 109 as `reason=unknown`; the new pre-flight check should make any
  remaining 101 stand out.
* The reason for the 04:51:24.682 to 04:51:31.827 gap: whether the server closed the
  socket with a frame or reset it. The close type is not recorded.
